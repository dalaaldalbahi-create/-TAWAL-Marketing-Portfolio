# Campaign concept: Behind the Network

**For:** TAWAL Marketing & Customer Engagement Plan  
**Proposed pilot:** Four LinkedIn posts during November 2026  
**Status:** Student proposal; not an official or approved TAWAL campaign.

## The idea

Help people understand the physical infrastructure behind everyday connectivity and business operations. Each post explains one point in plain language: what a tower company does, how sharing can work, why buildings may need indoor coverage solutions, and how an organization can start a relevant discussion.

The campaign should make infrastructure understandable without implying that TAWAL is a mobile network operator or promising network performance outcomes. TAWAL describes its services as including tower infrastructure, colocation, in-building solutions, and other related offerings. Final post copy should be checked against the current official service information before use. [1] [2]

## Audience

The first audience is network and connectivity teams. The second is property owners, venue managers, government bodies, and large-project stakeholders who may need to understand infrastructure services.

## Key message

> The services we use every day depend on physical digital infrastructure. TAWAL provides infrastructure and related services that organizations can use to support connectivity.

## Four content themes

1. **The physical side of connectivity:** A simple, labelled illustration of a telecom site and what the infrastructure provider manages.
2. **Sharing a site:** A plain-language introduction to colocation, without unsupported cost or coverage claims.
3. **Inside the building:** What an in-building solution is intended to address and which stakeholders may be involved.
4. **Start with the need:** A short checklist—location, type of need, and project stage—followed by a link to an approved information or contact page.

## Channels and formats

Use TAWAL’s LinkedIn page for one post each week. Use text with a simple illustration, a short carousel, a clear explainer graphic, and a checklist post. Link to an existing official solution or contact page. Build a separate landing page only if the organization has capacity to maintain it.

## Draft post examples

**1 — The network has a physical side**  
“Connectivity has a physical side. TAWAL designs, builds and manages telecom infrastructure that organizations can use to support connectivity. Learn how shared infrastructure works: [approved link].”

**2 — One site, shared use**  
“Colocation means more than one customer can use space on a telecom site. What does that mean for an organization exploring infrastructure options? See TAWAL’s colocation information: [approved link].”

**3 — Coverage inside the building**  
“Indoor coverage needs can differ from outdoor coverage needs. TAWAL describes In-Building Solutions for places such as offices, hotels, and public venues. Explore the service: [approved link].”

**4 — Start with the right question**  
“Planning an infrastructure project? Start with the location, the need, and the project stage. TAWAL’s team can point you to the relevant service information: [approved contact link].”

All examples are drafts. They are not approved TAWAL copy. Replace bracketed links with current official pages, and obtain technical and communications review before publishing. Do not insert a customer, result, savings, coverage, or sustainability claim unless it is verified and approved.

## Call to action

“Explore TAWAL’s solutions” or “Contact TAWAL to discuss your infrastructure requirement.” Use a tagged link if analytics are available.

## How to evaluate the pilot

Record impressions or reach, reactions, comments, shares, link clicks, and any available website sessions for each post. Compare the pilot with the four weeks before it using the same metric definitions. Count an inquiry only when it can be linked to the campaign and meets the organization’s normal qualification process. Report results internally; do not publish confidential lead information.
